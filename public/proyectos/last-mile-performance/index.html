Measuring Real vs. Theoretical Route Time for a Last-Mile Delivery Fleet

A route-performance and inventory management platform built for a mass-consumption distribution company running its own last-mile delivery fleet — sales reps, drivers, and warehouse staff working off three disconnected spreadsheets before this existed.

Route Benchmarking Inventory Management Python / Streamlit Role-Based PWA

The situation

Nobody could tell a late delivery from a badly planned route

The fleet ran on driver memory and paper logs. When a delivery ran late, there was no way to tell whether it was a bad route, a slow stop, or genuine traffic — because nothing recorded the theoretical time a route should have taken against what actually happened.

Inventory tracking sat in a separate system entirely, so a stockout at one distribution point and a delayed delivery at another looked like two unrelated problems, even when they had the same root cause.

What was built

One platform, four roles, one shared source of truth

Route benchmarking engine

Computes theoretical vs. actual route duration and distance for every delivery run, so "this route is slow" becomes a measured gap instead of a driver's opinion.

Role-based access for the whole chain

Admin, sales rep, logistics manager, and owner each see the view relevant to them — the driver logs a delivery from the field, the manager sees fleet-wide KPIs, the owner sees business-level rollups.

Unified inventory and client data layer

Client master data, product catalog, and delivery history live in one fast local data pipeline (pandas + JSON, cached) instead of three disconnected spreadsheets.

Executive-facing dashboard

Delivery completion rate, order volume, and per-driver route efficiency in one interactive view — the numbers an owner actually checks weekly.

Why it matters to a client

Backtracking and phantom "traffic delays" get named for what they are

Any business running its own delivery fleet — distributors, wholesalers, regional couriers — hits the same wall: without a theoretical-vs-actual benchmark, every late delivery gets blamed on traffic instead of route design. This platform gives owners and logistics managers the same visibility a much larger enterprise TMS would, built to the size of the actual fleet.
